Dear Sir/Madam,

I am writing to give formal notice of my intention to bring legal proceedings against your organisation for false imprisonment, breach of human rights, and negligence arising from my detention at Ellingham Hospital between approximately 2014 and 2019, when I was a child aged 14-17.

This letter is sent in accordance with the Pre-Action Protocol for Personal Injury Claims and the Pre-Action Protocol for Judicial Review.

BACKGROUND:

I was detained at Ellingham Hospital for approximately three years (2014-2019). I was a child throughout this period. I was never provided with:
- Mental Health Act section papers
- Secure accommodation orders
- Notice of my right to apply to the Mental Health Tribunal
- Notice of my nearest relative's rights
- Information about legal representation
- Capacity assessments
- Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards documentation

I was never informed of the legal basis for my detention. I was never told I had the right to challenge it. I was treated as lacking capacity without any formal assessment or legal process.

LEGAL BASIS OF CLAIM:

1. FALSE IMPRISONMENT

False imprisonment is the unlawful detention of a person without lawful authority or consent. If my detention lacked valid legal authority at any point during the three-year period, each day of unlawful detention constitutes a separate tort.

Given that I was never provided with section papers, secure accommodation orders, or any legal documentation, I have reasonable grounds to believe that my detention was unlawful for all or part of the period.

2. BREACH OF ARTICLE 5 ECHR (RIGHT TO LIBERTY AND SECURITY)

Article 5(1) of the European Convention on Human Rights provides that no one shall be deprived of their liberty save in accordance with a procedure prescribed by law. Article 5(4) provides the right to have the lawfulness of detention decided by a court.

If my detention was not authorised by valid legal process, or if I was denied the right to challenge my detention, this constitutes a breach of Article 5.

3. BREACH OF ARTICLE 8 ECHR (RIGHT TO RESPECT FOR PRIVATE AND FAMILY LIFE)

My detention for three years as a child, separated from my family and community, and without lawful authority, constitutes a serious interference with my right to private and family life.

4. BREACH OF ARTICLE 3 ECHR (PROHIBITION OF INHUMAN OR DEGRADING TREATMENT)

If the conditions of detention, the denial of legal rights, or the treatment I received amounted to inhuman or degrading treatment, this constitutes a breach of Article 3.

5. NEGLIGENCE

If the responsible authorities failed in their duty of care to me as a child in their custody, including:
- Failing to ensure lawful authority for detention
- Failing to inform me of my rights
- Failing to provide tribunal access
- Failing to provide appropriate treatment
- Failing to plan for my transition to adulthood

Then they are liable for negligence.

6. BREACH OF CHILDREN ACT 1989 DUTIES

As a looked-after child, the local authority owed me duties under the Children Act 1989, including:
- Duty to safeguard and promote my welfare (Section 17)
- Duty to provide accommodation (Section 20)
- Duty to provide leaving care support (Section 23)
- Duty to provide pathway plans (Section 23A)

If these duties were breached, the local authority is liable.

7. BREACH OF MENTAL HEALTH ACT 1983 DUTIES

If I was detained under the Mental Health Act 1983, the responsible authorities owed me duties including:
- Duty to provide section papers (Section 6)
- Duty to inform me of my rights (Section 132)
- Duty to provide tribunal access (Section 66)
- Duty to provide after-care (Section 117)

If these duties were breached, the responsible authorities are liable.

EVIDENCE:

I have recently obtained a medical report from Ellingham Hospital (page 348 of 357, dated 12 September 2017). I have made Subject Access Requests to Ellingham Hospital and Norfolk County Council for my full records. I reserve the right to amend and expand my claim upon receipt of these records.

INJURY AND LOSS:

The unlawful detention and breach of my rights has caused:
- Loss of liberty for approximately three years
- Psychological trauma and ongoing mental health impact
- Loss of educational and developmental opportunities
- Loss of family relationships and social development
- Ongoing distrust of authority and institutional systems
- Current homelessness and housing instability (directly linked to failure to provide after-care and transition support)
- Financial losses (including costs of care, accommodation, and legal proceedings)

I reserve the right to claim damages for:
- False imprisonment (per day of unlawful detention)
- Breach of human rights
- Negligence
- Special damages (financial losses)
- Aggravated and exemplary damages (for oppressive and arbitrary conduct)

TIME LIMIT:

I acknowledge that the standard limitation period for false imprisonment is six years. I was released in approximately 2019. The limitation period would ordinarily expire in 2025.

However, I submit that the limitation period should be extended because:
1. I was a child when detained and for a period after release
2. I was never informed of my legal rights or the basis for my detention
3. The responsible authorities withheld legal documentation from me
4. I have been pursuing other remedies (housing appeals, subject access requests) without legal representation
5. I have only recently discovered the extent of the legal breaches
6. The responsible authorities declared me lacking capacity, creating a barrier to my ability to instruct solicitors

I also submit that any Human Rights Act claim is subject to the court's discretion to extend time limits under Section 7(5)(b) of the Human Rights Act 1998.

DEMANDS:

I demand the following within 14 days of this letter:

1. Confirmation of the legal basis for my detention at Ellingham Hospital for the entire period 2014-2019, with copies of all relevant legal documentation.

2. Confirmation of whether Mental Health Tribunal reviews were conducted, with copies of all tribunal decisions.

3. Confirmation of whether Section 117 after-care was provided after my discharge, with copies of all after-care plans.

4. Confirmation of whether Children Act 1989 pathway plans and leaving care support were provided.

5. An interim payment of compensation pending full assessment of my claim.

6. A formal apology for the unlawful detention and breach of my rights.

If you fail to respond satisfactorily within 14 days, I will have no alternative but to:

1. Issue proceedings for false imprisonment in the High Court
2. Issue proceedings for breach of human rights under the Human Rights Act 1998
3. Issue proceedings for negligence
4. Apply for judicial review of any ongoing failures
5. Report the matter to the Care Quality Commission (CQC)
6. Report the matter to the Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman
7. Report the matter to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) for data protection breaches
8. Apply to the European Court of Human Rights if domestic remedies are exhausted

I am a litigant in person. I request that you do not take advantage of my lack of legal representation and that you engage with this claim in good faith.
