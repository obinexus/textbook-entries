PART 4: THE CLAIMANT'S GROUNDS FOR SEEKING JUDICIAL REVIEW

GROUND 1: ERROR OF LAW – MISAPPLICATION OF THE BEGUM TEST

The Defendant erred in law in its application of the test established in R v Tower Hamlets LBC ex p Ferdous Begum [1993] AC 509. The Begum test requires that a person lacks "serious capacity" to understand and respond to an offer of accommodation before a local authority may refuse to accept a homelessness application. The Defendant's own decision letter acknowledges this high threshold yet fails to apply it.

The Claimant:
- Holds a Bachelor's degree from a UK university;
- Is a member of British Mensa (high IQ society);
- Secured admission to Imperial College London for Master's studies (commencing October 2025);
- Successfully managed independent living in Kent from age 18;
- Has designed and documented complex technical systems (publicly available on GitHub under the OBINexus organisation);
- Has navigated university admissions, benefits systems (Universal Credit, PIP), and legal processes independently.

These achievements demonstrate a level of intellectual functioning, abstract reasoning, and long-term planning that far exceeds the complexity of understanding a tenancy agreement. The Defendant's conclusion that the Claimant lacks "serious capacity" is legally unsustainable and constitutes a misapplication of the Begum test.

GROUND 2: PROCEDURAL UNFAIRNESS – FAILURE TO CONDUCT A FORMAL CAPACITY ASSESSMENT

The Defendant failed to conduct a formal, structured assessment of the Claimant's capacity under the Mental Capacity Act 2005. The decision relies on:
- A 30-minute Microsoft Teams interview conducted on 5 August 2025;
- Emails from the Claimant's support provider (Heathcare Services);
- A general mental health assessment by Redbridge Access, Assessment & Brief Intervention Team dated 25 July 2025, which did not assess capacity for housing decisions.

Section 3 of the Mental Capacity Act 2005 requires that a person be unable to (a) understand relevant information, (b) retain it, (c) use or weigh it, and (d) communicate the decision. The Defendant did not apply this structured test. No capacity assessment specific to housing decisions was conducted by a qualified professional. The decision is procedurally defective and unlawful.

GROUND 3: FAILURE TO CONSIDER RELEVANT EVIDENCE

The Defendant failed to consider overwhelming evidence of the Claimant's capacity, including:

(a) Educational and intellectual achievements:
- Bachelor's degree completion;
- British Mensa membership;
- Oxford programme attendance through Newham City Council;
- Imperial College London Master's admission.

(b) Independent living history:
- Managed independent living in Kent from age 18;
- Navigated benefits systems (Universal Credit £232/month, PIP £732/month);
- Engaged with support services and legal processes.

(c) Technical and creative work:
- Design and documentation of complex digital infrastructure (OBINexus ecosystem);
- Mastery of multiple programming languages (Rust, JavaScript, TypeScript, Python);
- Creation of technical specifications for distributed systems with 7-layer architecture.

This evidence directly demonstrates the Claimant's ability to understand complex information, retain it, weigh it, and communicate decisions — the core requirements of the Mental Capacity Act 2005. The Defendant's failure to consider this evidence constitutes a serious error of law and fact.

GROUND 4: DISCRIMINATION – CONFLATION OF NEURODIVERGENCE WITH INCAPACITY

The Defendant conflated the Claimant's neurodivergent conditions (Autism Spectrum Disorder, ADHD) and mental health diagnoses with a lack of capacity. The decision:
- Cites the Claimant's Autism and "Unspecified Non-Organic Psychosis" as evidence of impairment;
- Uses the Claimant's non-compliance with prescribed medication (Sodium Valproate, Aripiprazole) as evidence of lack of insight;
- Characterises the Claimant's self-advocacy and insistence on independent living as evidence of incapacity;
- Fails to make reasonable adjustments for the Claimant's neurodivergent communication style during the 5 August 2025 interview.

This constitutes direct and indirect discrimination under the Equality Act 2010 and breaches the public sector equality duty under Section 149 of that Act. The Defendant has discriminated against the Claimant by treating his neurodivergence as incapacity rather than as a protected characteristic requiring reasonable adjustments.

GROUND 5: IRRATIONALITY – A DECISION NO REASONABLE AUTHORITY COULD HAVE REACHED

The decision is so unreasonable that no reasonable authority, properly directing itself on the law and the evidence, could have reached it. The Claimant:
- Has demonstrated capacity in complex, high-stakes decisions (university admissions, technical system design, benefits management);
- Has a documented history of independent living;
- Was declared to have capacity by Redbridge Access, Assessment & Brief Intervention Team in July 2025 (albeit for a different decision);
- Was subjected to a 30-minute video interview with no reasonable adjustments, no advocate present, and no formal assessment protocol.

A reasonable authority, faced with this evidence, could not have concluded that the Claimant lacks "serious capacity" for housing decisions. The decision is Wednesbury unreasonable.

PART 7: OUT-OF-TIME APPLICATION

The Claimant applies for permission to bring this claim out of time.

The time limit for judicial review is 3 months from the date of the decision (CPR 54.5). The decision was made on 22 August 2025. The Claimant is bringing this claim approximately 11 months out of time.

The Claimant respectfully submits that there is good reason for the delay:

(1) VULNERABILITY AND LACK OF CAPACITY TO INSTRUCT SOLICITORS: The Claimant was a vulnerable care leaver with diagnosed disabilities, placed in supported living, and declared lacking capacity by the very authority he needed to challenge. This declaration created a systemic barrier to accessing legal advice, as solicitors were reluctant to act for a person formally declared lacking capacity.

(2) PURSUIT OF INTERNAL REMEDIES: The Claimant pursued internal appeals, subject access requests, and pre-action protocol correspondence from September 2025 to June 2026, believing (reasonably) that the Defendant would correct its unlawful decision. The Defendant failed to do so.

(3) DISRUPTION OF PLACEMENTS: The Claimant was moved between supported living placements (from 15 Evesham Way, Ilford, to 4 Raymond Road, Ilford) without continuity of support. This disrupted his ability to maintain consistent legal advocacy and access to resources.

(4) LACK OF LEGAL REPRESENTATION: The Claimant has been unable to secure legal aid or pro bono representation due to his complex circumstances, limited financial resources, and the Defendant's capacity declaration, which deterred legal professionals from acting.

(5) CATCH-22 OF SELF-ADVOCACY: The Claimant's attempts to advocate for himself — including his technical work, educational achievements, and insistence on his rights — were used by the Defendant as evidence of "lack of insight" and incapacity. This created a perverse incentive where the Claimant's strengths were weaponised against him, discouraging further self-advocacy and delaying formal legal action.

(6) NO PREJUDICE TO THE DEFENDANT: The delay has caused no prejudice to the Defendant. The Defendant has not relied on the delay to its detriment. The facts and evidence remain unchanged. The Claimant's circumstances (lack of secure housing, vulnerability) remain urgent.

The Claimant submits that the overriding objective (CPR 1.1) and the interests of justice require that this claim be permitted to proceed out of time. The Defendant's decision is fundamentally unlawful, and denying the Claimant access to justice would compound the injustice he has already suffered.
